Dynamic Model Routing: Cutting LLM Spend Without Cutting Capability

Most enterprise LLM spend isn't a model-selection problem; it's a routing problem. A reference architecture for AI gateways and the four places they break.

Dynamic model routing is the architectural discipline of dispatching inference requests to the most cost-effective LLM tier capable of solving the specific task. Most enterprise LLM spend isn't a model-selection problem; it's a routing problem that inflates cloud bills when uniform frontier models process trivial tasks.

Organisations don't discover this immediately. A team ships something useful on a frontier model. Adoption spreads. Six months later the monthly invoice is an agenda item, and the first instinct is to swap the model for a cheaper one across the board. Quality drops, the team swaps back, and the conclusion is that cost is simply the price of capability.

That conclusion is wrong, because it treats a heterogeneous workload as a single decision. A governed gateway that routes each request to the cheapest model *capable of completing that specific task* changes the economics without touching the ceiling on quality.

This is a reference architecture for that gateway. It describes what components do, what the non-obvious failure modes are, and what to measure. You can't benchmark routing outcomes in the abstract: results depend entirely on your prompt distribution.
