Post-Quantum Cryptographic Readiness for Legacy Infrastructure

Post-quantum readiness is an inventory challenge before a cryptography issue. Use Mosca's inequality to prioritize data and build lasting crypto-agility.

Post-quantum cryptographic readiness is an enterprise inventory challenge before it is a mathematical upgrade. You can't migrate cryptography you can't find. In most production estates, automated asset discovery and lifecycle classification take significantly longer than swapping the underlying algorithms.

Post-quantum readiness is usually discussed as a cryptography problem, which is why it usually stalls. The algorithms are standardised and available. What isn't available, in almost any large organisation, is a reliable answer to a simpler question: where is cryptography used, by which systems, with which key material, and who owns each instance?

That question is answerable. It's also unglamorous, largely a discovery and asset-management exercise, and it determines whether your actual migration takes quarters or years.

Most cryptographic risk is prospective; it materialises when the attack becomes feasible. Harvest-now-decrypt-later inverts that. An adversary captures encrypted traffic today, stores it, and decrypts it when capable hardware exists.
