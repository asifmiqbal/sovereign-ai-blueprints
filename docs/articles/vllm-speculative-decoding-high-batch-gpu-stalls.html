vLLM Speculative Decoding Stalls: The High-Batch GPU Wall

Why 5-token speculative decoding speeds up low-batch LLM queries by 2.6x but spikes P99 latency by 183% once GPU batch concurrency crosses 16.

Imagine hiring a fast junior typist to guess and type five sentences ahead while a senior editor reviews all five at once. With one manuscript on the desk, turnaround time drops in half. Now imagine 48 manuscripts land on the desk simultaneously: crossing out four wrong guesses per page buries the editor under 240 wasted paragraphs, stalling the entire room. That's the exact hardware physics behind high-batch speculative decoding stalls on production NVIDIA H100 and B200 GPU clusters.

Every AI infrastructure team has experienced this production whiplash. You benchmark a 70-billion-parameter model (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) paired with a 1-billion-parameter draft model (K = 5 lookahead tokens) on a quiet staging server at Batch Size 1. Why does an optimization designed to accelerate decoding actively choke your GPUs under real traffic? Because speculative decoding isn't free: it trades idle arithmetic FLOPs for saved memory bandwidth. If you don't disable it when batch concurrency rises, it's guaranteed to saturate your Tensor Cores.
