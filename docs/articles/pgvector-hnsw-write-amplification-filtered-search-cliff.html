The 'Just Use Postgres' Vector Cliff: Where pgvector HNSW Breaks at 50M Rows

Monolithic Postgres pgvector HNSW indexes hit write-amplification and VACUUM stalls past 50M rows, where hash-partitioned halfvec and DiskANN take over.

Consolidating vector search onto PostgreSQL with pgvector simplifies stack architecture up to a few million rows, but monolithic in-memory HNSW indexes hit a steep operational cliff between 20 million and 50 million embeddings. At that scale, MVCC tuple churn triggers heavy HNSW graph write-amplification and VACUUM stalls, while selective multi-tenant WHERE filters disconnect graph entry points and collapse search recall.

Over the past two years, "Just use Postgres" became the default architectural mantra across Hacker News and enterprise engineering blogs. Instead of spinning up a separate dedicated vector database alongside a primary relational store, teams enabled pgvector inside their existing PostgreSQL clusters to keep ACID transactions, relational joins, and embeddings in one place.

At two million vectors, that consolidation feels unbeatable. Queries return in 8ms, backups are unified, and ops overhead is zero. Then your enterprise document ingestion pipeline scales to 50 million 1536-dimensional embeddings with continuous streaming updates and row-level security filters. Suddenly, replication lag spikes to forty minutes, autovacuum pins NVMe disk IOPS at 100%, and tenant-filtered vector queries don't even return ten results. It's a classic physics trap: you can't treat a multi-layer proximity graph like a standard B-tree index at fifty-million-row scale.

PostgreSQL pgvector HNSW hits a performance cliff at 50 million rows because Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs must reside in RAM (shared_buffers) to avoid random disk seeks during greedy traversal. At 50 million 1536-dimensional float32 vectors, the HNSW index exceeds 580 GB, triggering severe disk page thrashing on every concurrent upsert and VACUUM pass.
