Inside the $6.36B Binance Heist: What We Know (And What Still Doesn't Add Up)

On-chain forensics show how the $6.36B Binance heist used compromised containers and EIP-7702 delegations to drain 10 chains in 20 minutes without stealing keys.

On October 29, 2026, state-sponsored hackers drained $6.36 billion from Binance across 10 blockchains in 20 minutes—the largest digital bank heist in history—without stealing a single private key. Instead of cracking wallet encryption, the attackers compromised internal service containers and pushed 50 pre-signed EIP-7702 delegations in one transaction, tricking the signing server into handing full control of the hot wallet to an automated sweeper contract.

Imagine a bank builds an unbreakable steel vault. Nobody can drill through the door, and the secret stamp inside never leaves the room.

So how do thieves empty the vault in 20 minutes? They don't attack the vault door. Instead, they compromise the internal messenger clerk who slips paperwork through the slot, and they trick that clerk into sliding a Power of Attorney form under the stamp. Once the machine inside blindly stamps *"APPROVED,"* the thieves control the account.
