The Vector Residency Trap: Why Unencrypted Embeddings Violate PDPA

Vec2Text inversion models reconstruct 78% of raw PII from unencrypted float embeddings. Keyed orthogonal projections enforce ASEAN PDPA and GDPR residency.

Many enterprise architectures keep primary SQL databases inside domestic borders to satisfy data residency laws, yet replicate unencrypted 1536-dimensional vector embeddings to managed vector databases in foreign cloud regions. Because iterative embedding inversion models (Vec2Text) can reconstruct 78% to 92% of original plaintext PII directly from floating-point vectors, unencrypted embeddings legally constitute personal data under Malaysia's PDPA, Singapore's PDPA, and the EU GDPR.

Across Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Bangkok, and Frankfurt, enterprise architecture diagrams repeat the same dangerous assumption. To pass a local data residency audit, the CISO mandates that core PostgreSQL tables and customer document buckets remain pinned inside a domestic data center. However, because the AI engineering team wants low-latency global search or managed cloud convenience, they ship the resulting dense vector embeddings (float32[1536]) to a centralized US-East or EU-West vector database.

When compliance officers ask whether sensitive data is crossing borders, developers confidently reply: *"No, raw text stays in-country; we only export arrays of floating-point numbers."* That defense doesn't hold up under modern cryptanalysis. As we outlined in our [sovereign data substrates](/articles/sovereign-data-substrates-cloud-act) and [zero-trust data substrates](/articles/zero-trust-data-substrates) research, a dense text embedding isn't a one-way cryptographic hash. It's a reversible compression of the exact source text.

Attackers can reconstruct raw PII from vector database embeddings using iterative controlled-decoding inversion models (Vec2Text) that query the embedding space and refine candidate text until its vector matches the target float array. Across standard 768-dimensional and 1536-dimensional enterprise embeddings, 50 steps of corrective inversion recover 78.4% of exact tokens and 92.1% of full customer names, NRIC/passport numbers, and clinical diagnoses.
